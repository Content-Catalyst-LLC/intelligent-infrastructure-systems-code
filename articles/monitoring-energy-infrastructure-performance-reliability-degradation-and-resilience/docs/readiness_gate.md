# Deployment Readiness Gate

Before energy performance monitoring workflows are used for operations, maintenance prioritization, outage review, regulatory reporting, resilience planning, capital investment, predictive maintenance, or public claims, they should pass a readiness gate.

1. Asset classes, service obligations, monitoring goals, decision uses, and valid-use limits are defined.
2. Assets are linked to network role, location, age, criticality, owner, operator, and service consequence.
3. Latency, missingness, calibration, timestamp quality, sensor status, and provenance are tracked.
4. Reliability, degradation, power-quality, efficiency, and resilience metrics are defined, tested, and documented.
5. Performance alerts are connected to inspection, work orders, dispatch changes, outage response, or capital planning.
6. Access controls, segmentation, logging, failover, manual fallback, and incident response procedures are defined.
7. Monitoring supports stress scenarios, recovery metrics, restoration sequencing, and fallback capability.
8. Assumptions, limitations, responsible agencies, review cycles, and public claims are documented.
