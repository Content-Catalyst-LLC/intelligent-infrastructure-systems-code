# Engineering Notes

## Design priorities

1. Define mobility goals before selecting technology.
2. Preserve multimodal context: roads, transit, freight, curb, walking, cycling, public space, and emergency access.
3. Measure reliability and accessibility, not only speed or throughput.
4. Track data quality, latency, location validity, missingness, and provenance.
5. Evaluate vulnerable users, disability access, fare/payment inclusion, and digital access.
6. Document cybersecurity, failover, manual operations, and incident response.
7. Connect analytics to dispatch, signal operations, transit control, maintenance, public communication, and investment.

## Common failure modes

- vehicle-flow optimization degrading pedestrian safety or bus reliability
- dashboards without operational response authority
- digital ticketing excluding cash-dependent or smartphone-limited users
- freight and curb conflicts omitted from smart mobility plans
- proprietary platforms creating vendor lock-in
- real-time data used without quality flags
- digital systems lacking manual fallback
