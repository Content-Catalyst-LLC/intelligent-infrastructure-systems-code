# Engineering Notes

## Design priorities

1. Define service, quality, public-health, resilience, and governance questions before selecting indicators.
2. Preserve asset, service-zone, hydraulic-zone, water-quality, wastewater, stormwater, and public-health context.
3. Measure quality compliance, pressure adequacy, leakage, service continuity, observability, wastewater risk, stormwater exposure, and resilience.
4. Track data quality, timestamps, latency, missingness, calibration, sampling coverage, laboratory confirmation, units, metadata, and provenance.
5. Distinguish sensor signal, laboratory evidence, hydraulic behavior, customer complaint, field inspection, model output, and governance decision.
6. Document SCADA, remote access, field devices, credentials, firmware, cybersecurity, failover, manual operations, and incident response.
7. Connect indicators to treatment adjustment, field dispatch, work orders, public notification, compliance reporting, emergency response, or capital planning.

## Common failure modes

- digital water is treated as a dashboard project rather than operational governance
- quality indicators are not linked to sampling and laboratory evidence
- pressure and leakage metrics lack hydraulic-zone context
- wastewater and stormwater risks remain isolated from water-system operations
- telemetry systems lack calibration, metadata, or latency tracking
- SCADA and field devices lack cybersecurity governance
- alerts are not linked to action, public communication, or review closure
