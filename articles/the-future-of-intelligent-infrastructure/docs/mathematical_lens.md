# Mathematical Lens

Future intelligent infrastructure should be evaluated by systems capability rather than technology deployment counts.

## Observability

\[
O = \frac{N_{\mathrm{observable\ critical\ assets}}}{N_{\mathrm{critical\ assets}}}
\]

Observability measures the share of critical assets with usable current-state evidence, such as telemetry, inspections, condition records, or validated monitoring.

## Interoperability

\[
I_{\mathrm{interop}} =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{systems\ with\ shared\ identifiers}}}{N_{\mathrm{core\ systems}}}
\]

Interoperability improves when core systems exchange data through shared identifiers, documented schemas, and governed interfaces.

## Infrastructure risk

\[
R = P_{\mathrm{failure}} \times C_{\mathrm{consequence}}
\]

Infrastructure risk can be approximated as failure probability multiplied by consequence, while recognizing uncertainty, dependencies, and distributional impacts.

## Adaptive capacity

\[
A =
\frac{N_{\mathrm{adaptive\ decisions}}}{N_{\mathrm{material\ stress\ events}}}
\]

Adaptive capacity can be assessed by whether institutions update operations, maintenance, investment, or response after material stress events.

## Cyber-resilience readiness

\[
S_{\mathrm{secure}} =
w_1C_{\mathrm{seg}} +
w_2C_{\mathrm{access}} +
w_3C_{\mathrm{recovery}} +
w_4C_{\mathrm{monitoring}}
\]

Cyber-resilience readiness depends on segmentation, access controls, recovery capability, and security monitoring.

## Infrastructure intelligence quality

\[
Q_{\mathrm{intelligence}} =
w_1O +
w_2I_{\mathrm{interop}} +
w_3A +
w_4R_{\mathrm{resilience}} +
w_5E +
w_6G +
w_7S_{\mathrm{secure}}
\]

Infrastructure intelligence quality combines observability, interoperability, adaptability, resilience, equity, governance, and security.
