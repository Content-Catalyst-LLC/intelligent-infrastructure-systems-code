# Deployment Readiness Gate

Before urban resilience analytics are used for public reporting, capital planning, emergency management, adaptation strategy, infrastructure investment, service-continuity claims, or equity review, they should pass a readiness gate.

1. Acute shocks, chronic stresses, affected zones, and scenario assumptions are documented.
2. Essential services, continuity targets, responsible agencies, and restoration priorities are defined.
3. Power, water, drainage, transport, communications, housing, health, and emergency dependencies are mapped.
4. Exposure, social vulnerability, service access, and recovery capacity are assessed by population and place.
5. Redundancy, backup systems, recovery-time objectives, restoration sequencing, and drills are documented.
6. Green infrastructure, land-use controls, flood storage, canopy, and ecological buffers are documented and maintained.
7. Authorities, funding, maintenance responsibilities, update cycles, and public reporting are defined.
8. After-action reviews, corrective actions, and adaptive pathways are updated after disruptions and new evidence.
