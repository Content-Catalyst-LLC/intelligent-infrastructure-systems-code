# Engineering Notes

## Design priorities

1. Define reliability, resilience, flexibility, public-service, and governance questions before selecting indicators.
2. Preserve topology, service-zone, asset-class, DER, telemetry, control-system, and cyber-physical context.
3. Measure grid observability, voltage adequacy, flexibility adequacy, balancing pressure, service continuity, cyber-physical risk, and resilience.
4. Track timestamps, latency, missingness, time synchronization, calibration, device health, metadata, units, and provenance.
5. Distinguish sensor signal, operational state, model output, field evidence, outage event, DER program status, and governance decision.
6. Document SCADA, AMI, DERMS, OMS, remote access, field devices, credentials, firmware, cybersecurity, failover, manual operations, and incident response.
7. Connect indicators to dispatch, voltage control, outage restoration, field response, public communication, regulatory reporting, or capital planning.

## Common failure modes

- smart grid modernization is treated as a device deployment rather than operational governance
- telemetry lacks topology and service-zone context
- distributed energy resources exist nominally but are not visible or dispatchable
- voltage, frequency, loading, and balancing risk are not connected to field action
- cybersecurity is treated as an IT issue rather than a reliability issue
- automation lacks fallback and degraded-mode procedures
- public claims are not supported by auditable evidence chains
