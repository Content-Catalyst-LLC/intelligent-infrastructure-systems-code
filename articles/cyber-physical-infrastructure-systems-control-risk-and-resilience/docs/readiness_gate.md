# Deployment Readiness Gate

Before cyber-physical infrastructure workflows are used for operations, automated control, remote intervention, safety response, resilience scoring, public reporting, regulatory claims, predictive maintenance, dispatch, outage restoration, water-system control, grid reliability, traffic management, building automation, or incident response, they should pass a readiness gate.

1. Operational goals, service objectives, control boundaries, valid uses, prohibited uses, and responsible owners are defined.
2. Physical assets, sensors, actuators, controllers, gateways, networks, software, vendors, and dependencies are documented.
3. Signals are calibrated, timestamped, quality-flagged, authenticated, and valid for intended decision use.
4. Control logic, thresholds, setpoints, timing constraints, safety boundaries, and intervention pathways are tested.
5. Remote access, credentials, network zones, firmware, logs, APIs, and vendor pathways are protected and monitored.
6. Operators understand system state, automation status, alert meaning, override options, and escalation paths.
7. The system can degrade safely, isolate affected components, continue critical functions, and recover service.
8. Assumptions, limitations, decision authority, incident reporting, service claims, and review cycles are documented.
